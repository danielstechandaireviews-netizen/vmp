Plenty of things help a channel grow, but three rules matter more than any trick or trend.

## 1. Originality

YouTube's monetization policies require content to be **original and authentic**. Reused content (other people's clips with little added) and mass-produced, repetitive videos are the most common reasons channels are turned down for the YouTube Partner Program, even after they reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours.

Using AI tools is fine. What matters is that every video adds your own script, ideas, commentary or story.

## 2. Consistency

Channels that upload on a steady schedule give viewers a reason to come back, and they give YouTube more signals about who their videos are for. Pick a pace you can keep, whether that's one or two videos a week, and stick to it for months, not days.

## 3. Educative content

Videos that teach something (how to do a task, why something happened, what changed) keep people watching longer and get searched for long after upload. Even entertainment channels grow faster when viewers learn something along the way.

**Today's takeaway:** before you publish your next video, ask three questions. Is it mine? Is it on schedule? Will the viewer learn something?
