Subscribers, views, likes and comments all feel like the same goal: "more". But they do different jobs, and only some of them count directly toward monetization:

| Metric | Counts toward YPP? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscribers** | ✅ Yes, you need 1,000 | A required threshold |
| **Views and watch time** | ✅ Yes: 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views (doubling Feb 1, 2027) | A required threshold |
| **Likes and comments** | ❌ Not directly | Signals that help YouTube recommend you, which drives the other two |

Here's how to grow each one in a way that sticks.

## Getting more subscribers

People subscribe when they expect *more of something they just enjoyed*. That means:

- **Make the channel about one thing.** "A channel about budgeting for students" earns subscribers. "A channel about whatever I'm into this week" doesn't.
- **Ask at the right moment.** Not in the first ten seconds, but right after you've delivered something useful: "If that helped, there's a new one every Tuesday."
- **Show what's coming.** A channel trailer, a clear banner and organised playlists tell visitors what they'll get by subscribing.
- **Turn Shorts viewers into subscribers.** Shorts reach new people fast. Point them to a playlist or a related long video.

## Getting more views

Views come from two places: being **clicked** and being **recommended**.

- **Title and thumbnail are one message.** The thumbnail shows the moment, the title adds the curiosity. Test a new thumbnail if click-through is low.
- **Win the first 15 seconds.** Retention in the opening is one of the strongest signals YouTube uses to keep recommending a video.
- **Search-friendly titles for evergreen topics.** "How to…" and "… explained" videos keep pulling in views for years.
- **Promote your best video, not your newest.** Pushing the video with the strongest retention gets more value from every new viewer.

## Getting more likes and comments

Likes and comments don't count toward the thresholds, but they tell YouTube that viewers care, and they build the community that keeps people coming back.

- **Ask a specific question.** "What do you think?" gets silence. "Which of these three would you pick?" gets answers.
- **Pin a comment** that starts the conversation.
- **Reply to early comments.** Replies in the first hours keep the discussion going and bring people back.
- **Use community posts and polls** between uploads.

## Why bought engagement backfires

It's tempting to "top up" with purchased subscribers, views, likes or comments. Here's what actually happens:

- **YouTube removes it.** Fake views and subscribers are regularly stripped out, so the numbers you paid for disappear.
- **It hurts real performance.** Bots don't watch, so your retention and click-through rates drop, and YouTube recommends you *less*.
- **It can sink your application.** Fake engagement breaks YouTube's rules and is a common reason channels are rejected from the Partner Program.
- **It can cost you the channel.** Serious or repeated violations can lead to termination.

The same goes for sub4sub groups, view exchanges and paying people to watch. Real viewers are the only engagement that lasts.
