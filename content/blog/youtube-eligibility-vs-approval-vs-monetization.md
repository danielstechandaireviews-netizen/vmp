"I hit 4,000 hours, so why am I not making money yet?"

We hear this every week. The answer is that YouTube monetization isn't one finish line. It's three separate stages, and each one is decided by something different:

| Stage | What it means | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Eligibility** | Your channel meets the numbers | Your audience |
| **2. Application** | You've applied and YouTube is reviewing | You (applying), then YouTube (reviewing) |
| **3. Monetization** | YouTube has accepted you into the Partner Program | YouTube |

Mixing these up is how creators end up surprised, and how some services end up promising things nobody can promise. Here's each stage in plain terms, and exactly where ViralMoniPoint (VMP) fits in.

## Stage 1: Eligibility (hitting the numbers)

Eligibility means your channel has reached YouTube's thresholds for the full Partner Program:

- **1,000 subscribers**, plus
- **4,000 public watch hours** from long-form videos in the last 365 days, **or 10 million public Shorts views** in the last 90 days

When you get there, the **Apply** button unlocks in YouTube Studio under **Earn**. That's all eligibility gets you: the right to apply.

Remember the deadline: from **February 1, 2027**, new applicants need **8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views**. The 1,000-subscriber rule stays the same. [Here's what's changing](/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements-2027).

**This is the stage our services are built around.** It's the part that comes down to work: the right niche, videos people actually watch, consistent uploads and smart packaging.

## Stage 2: Application (asking YouTube to review you)

Once you're eligible, you apply. In YouTube Studio you'll:

1. **Accept the Partner Program base terms**
2. **Set up or link an AdSense account** (one per person, and you must be 18 or older, or have a parent or guardian set it up)
3. **Submit for review**, after making sure 2-Step Verification is on and you have no active Community Guidelines strikes

Your channel then goes into review. YouTube looks at things like your channel's main theme, your most-viewed and newest videos, the videos with the most watch time, and your titles, thumbnails and descriptions. Reviewers are checking that the channel follows the **YouTube channel monetization policies**, especially the rules on **reused** and **inauthentic** (mass-produced or repetitive) content.

YouTube says most channels get a decision in **about a month**, though it can take longer. That's why we keep telling creators to **qualify and apply before the end of December 2026**: you want room for the review before February 1, 2027.

## Stage 3: Monetization (YouTube says yes, or no)

This is the decision. Two outcomes:

- **Approved.** You're in the Partner Program. You choose which ways to earn (such as ads on long-form videos and the Shorts revenue-sharing module), turn on ads, and start earning. Once in, you stay in as long as you follow the policies and keep the channel active.
- **Not approved.** YouTube tells you the policy it believes the channel doesn't meet. You can usually **reapply after 30 days**, once you've fixed the problem. That often means removing or reworking the videos that caused it.

Rejections are almost never about the numbers. They're about the *content*: reused clips with little original commentary, near-identical templated videos, or content that looks mass-produced. This is why **how** you reach eligibility matters as much as reaching it.

## Quick answers

**I'm eligible but haven't applied. Am I monetized?**
No. Eligibility only unlocks the Apply button. Nothing is reviewed until you apply.

**I applied before February 1, 2027. Do the new thresholds apply to me?**
Channels already in the Partner Program keep their status. If you're still waiting, apply as early as you can. Don't count on a review that's still open on February 1.

**My application was rejected. Is my channel ruined?**
No. Read the reason YouTube gives, fix the content it points to, and reapply when allowed. [Talk to us](/#contact) if you want help working out what to change.

**Do you guarantee I'll get monetized?**
We guarantee the work toward eligibility on Long-Form Monetization. Monetization itself is decided by YouTube, and we'll always say so plainly.
